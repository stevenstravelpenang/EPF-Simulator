Good to know
The 4% rule and "25x": what they mean
25x your yearly spending and the 4% rule are the same rule written two ways, because 1 ÷ 4% = 25.

Are they the same?

Yes, they are equivalent. The 4% rule says you can withdraw about 4% of your savings in your first year of retirement. Turn it around and ask "how big must my savings be?" and you get: yearly spending ÷ 0.04 = yearly spending × 25.

Example: you want RM 4,000 a month, which is RM 48,000 a year. RM 48,000 × 25 = RM 1,200,000. And 4% of RM 1,200,000 is RM 48,000. Same answer both ways.

Where it comes from

The 4% figure comes from US research in the 1990s (William Bengen in 1994, then the "Trinity Study" in 1998). They tested mixes of shares and bonds against past market history. Withdrawing 4% in year one, then raising the amount each year with inflation, lasted 30 years in nearly all the periods they tested.

Why this planner uses 25x

  • It is simple and widely known, and it gives one clear target number.
  • It does not depend on guessing future dividends. A 4% withdrawal is below EPF's 10-year average conventional dividend of about 5.9%, which leaves a cushion for inflation and weaker years.

Honest limits

  • It was built for about 30-year retirements using US data. If you retire early and need money for 40 years or more, many planners use 3% to 3.5% instead (about 29x to 33x).
  • EPF is not a plain stock-and-bond mix. The dividend is declared once a year and is only guaranteed at 2.5% for conventional savings.
  • The real 4% rule raises your withdrawals with inflation every year. This planner keeps your spending and the target in today's ringgit, so its results look kinder than real life. Treat them as a guide, not a promise.
This tool provides an illustrative projection only, based on the assumptions you enter and the fixed rates shown above, in today's ringgit (no inflation adjustment applied to spending or the target). If you set a planned retirement year or age, contributions are assumed to stop entirely from that point and your monthly spending is withdrawn from the EPF balance every year after, growing only by the dividend rate, until age 100 or until the balance reaches zero. The same applies automatically once your balance crosses the Rule of 25 target, even without a plan set. Extra spending is part of your retirement spending: from the start year you enter, the annual withdrawal equals (desired retirement spending + extra spending) x 12, until age 100. If no start year is entered, the extra spending is not counted. It only affects retirement years, so a start year before your retirement year takes effect at retirement. The Rule of 25 target always uses your desired retirement spending only. "EPF balance at retirement" is the balance you carry into your first retirement year, before that year's dividend and withdrawal. Voluntary top-ups are assumed to be paid in January of the year entered, added directly to that year's balance, and earn that full year's dividend; top-up years must fall after the current year. In every year, the contribution (working years) or withdrawal (retired years) and any voluntary top-up are applied to the balance first, and "Dividend received" is then calculated on that adjusted balance. The dividend history table is reference information only and does not change the projection; the dividend rate used in the projection is the one you enter. The red, yellow and green savings lines are fixed benchmarks of RM390,000, RM650,000 and RM1,300,000. It does not account for EPF withdrawals (housing, medical, Age 50/55 rules), tax, market volatility, or changes to EPF policy, and is not financial advice.
